# Eco-Guardian Community Pilot Proposal

## At a glance

Eco-Guardian is a community environmental engagement app combining sustainability
lessons, personal eco-activity tracking, weekly community challenges, events,
environmental incident reporting, and a marketplace for sustainable products.

This proposal outlines a small, time-limited pilot with one school, NGO,
business, or community group. It is a discussion draft, not a service contract,
pricing offer, or promise of independently verified environmental outcomes.

## Proposed pilot

- **Duration:** 6 weeks, following a short setup and orientation period.
- **Cohort:** One partner organization and approximately 20–50 voluntary adult
  participants. A smaller group is suitable for an initial usability test.
- **Primary question:** Does Eco-Guardian help participants learn about
  sustainability and consistently record practical environmental actions?
- **Format:** Participants use individual Eco-Guardian accounts. The partner
  promotes the pilot, helps recruit volunteers, and joins brief feedback
  check-ins.
- **Cost:** To be agreed before recruitment. The app has no published
  organization pricing or paid subscription at this time. Any pilot fee,
  expenses, support, or customization should be agreed in writing in advance.

## What participants can try

Participants can use the current app to:

- Complete sustainability lessons and quizzes.
- Log their own eco-actions and self-reported estimated CO₂ savings.
- Join the available weekly community challenges and track their own progress.
- Browse and RSVP to community events.
- Browse sustainable marketplace listings.
- Submit environmental incident reports.

Carbon savings are self-reported, not independently verified, and the app is
not a complete carbon-footprint calculator. Challenge participation is not
organization-specific: the current app does not provide private organization
workspaces, organization-scoped challenges, or organization dashboards.
Marketplace purchasing and media uploads also depend on the relevant Firebase,
Storage Rules, and payment configuration being deployed and tested. The pilot
should not depend on those capabilities unless they have first been verified.

## Suggested schedule

### Before launch

1. Confirm the partner, target participants, pilot dates, point of contact, and
   what success would mean to them.
2. Verify sign-up, data access rules, lessons, tracking, challenges, and any
   pilot features in the actual deployment.
3. Explain what data the app stores, what participation makes visible, and how
   to stop participating.
4. Recruit volunteers and provide a short orientation.

### Weeks 1–2: Onboarding and usability

- Invite participants to create accounts and explore the lessons and tracking.
- Ask participants to complete a short onboarding/usability survey.
- Record sign-up and first meaningful action completion.
- Fix blocking issues before encouraging broader use.

### Weeks 3–5: Continued participation

- Share one weekly reminder through the partner's existing communication
  channel; app notifications are not assumed to be available.
- Encourage participants to try lessons, log actions, or join a challenge.
- Hold a short weekly check-in with the partner contact.
- Gather optional feedback about usefulness, accessibility, and friction.

### Week 6: Review

- Send a short exit survey and invite optional interviews.
- Review aggregate participation and product reliability.
- Agree whether to stop, extend, run another pilot, or discuss a future paid
  offering.

## Measurement plan

Agree on the final targets with the partner before launch. The ranges below are
initial hypotheses for a small pilot, not guarantees or industry benchmarks.

| Measure | Definition | Initial hypothesis |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Activation | Participants who sign up and complete at least one lesson, challenge action, or other eco-action within 7 days ÷ invited participants | 50–70% |
| Week-4 engagement | Activated participants with at least one meaningful action during week 4 ÷ activated participants | 30–50% |
| Learning use | Participants who complete at least one lesson or quiz ÷ activated participants | Track baseline; set a target with the partner |
| Challenge participation | Participants who opt into a weekly challenge ÷ activated participants | Track baseline; improve after the first week |
| Retention | Participants who are active in the final pilot week ÷ activated participants | 25–40% |
| Reliability | Successful sign-ins and core actions, plus reported blocking failures | No unresolved blocker at pilot close |
| Satisfaction | Participants rating the app useful or very useful in the exit survey | 60% or more |
| Partner intent | Partner's stated willingness to continue, repeat, or pay for a defined future service | Record decision and reasons |

Measure activity separately from estimated impact. Do not present self-reported
CO₂ figures as audited savings, verified impact, or a complete footprint
calculation. Report participant counts and activity trends in aggregate wherever
possible.

## Data, consent, and reporting

- Participation should be voluntary, with a clear explanation of the app and
  data collected before people sign up.
- Use each participant's own account. Do not ask participants to share
  passwords.
- Do not give the organization access to private user profiles or individual
  progress. The current app has no organization dashboard or partner reporting
  access.
- Share only aggregate results with the partner, and suppress small-group
  breakdowns that could identify individuals.
- Do not collect sensitive information in surveys or incident reports unless
  it is necessary and the handling has been agreed.
- Agree in advance on survey storage, retention, deletion, and who can access
  the results.
- Give participants a contact for questions, accessibility needs, or account
  problems.

## What the partner provides

- A primary contact and a suitable participant group.
- A concise invitation and reminders through existing channels.
- Time for onboarding and short feedback sessions.
- Agreement on pilot objectives and success criteria.
- Timely reporting of bugs, accessibility barriers, or safety concerns.

## What the Eco-Guardian team provides

- A verified deployment and a guided orientation.
- A support contact and response expectations agreed before launch.
- A weekly aggregate progress summary, if the available data and consent
  support it; otherwise, a summary based on voluntary surveys.
- A closing report covering participation, feedback, reliability, limitations,
  and recommended next steps.

## Decision at the end

At the closing review, decide whether the evidence supports:

1. Ending the pilot with no further commitment.
2. Extending or repeating the pilot with improvements.
3. Building organization-specific workspaces and aggregate reporting before
   inviting more partners.
4. Discussing a future paid pilot or license with written scope, support,
   privacy terms, and pricing.

No future subscription, group workspace, organization analytics, payment
processing, or verified environmental certification is included unless it is
separately built, tested, and agreed in writing.
